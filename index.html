Holodeck · Training Program

Up-Rigger

The holodeck stays at its default until you pick a program at the arch. Then the grid gives way to an arena: a catwalk and a 24-inch ladder beam 108 feet over the floor, a ground rigger below building a bridle, another up-rigger on the beam across from you. You have a 150-foot rope, a progress capture device, and a job: get your leg of the bridle up, around the beam, mated, squared and checked — without dropping anything. Gravity is always on. Gravity is the opponent.

Two points on the same beam, 108 feet over the Moda Center floor. First a split 15 — a 5 and a 10 shackled together, the 5 wraps the beam, the 10 swings under and mates to the eye of the 5. Then a 10 basket — one 10 around the beam, both eyes in the bow. Gear up once; the rope comes back up between points.

Body

Quest exposes 83 body joints to the page; the legs are Meta's generative estimate, so your feet land about where they should. Off, the feet follow your head.

How far past the beam's edge your head (or a foot) can hang before you are off it. Real steel is zero.

Haul

Metres of rope per metre of arm travel. 1 is real hand-over-hand — about ninety pulls to the beam.

How fast the other up-rigger hauls once you are pulling together. Match it or the bridle drifts.

How heavy everything feels: the grip it takes and how fast your arm tires. 1 is the real numbers — a 1-ton motor with 60 feet of chain, 3/8" legs, 1/2" rope.

How quickly a loaded hand starts to shake and then lets go. 0 turns it off.

Bottom hook on the floor: 60 feet of chain leaves the bag on the way up and the load grows with height. Off, the chain rides in the bag.

Rope

How finely the 150 feet is simulated. Every inch is 1,800 particles — the most rope-like, and the most work for the headset. Applies when the program starts.

How finely the tube is drawn between particles.

Rope within about five feet of either hand is kept a rope's width apart from itself, with friction between the strands. Elsewhere it is free to pass through itself, which keeps the headset happy.

Look

The colour of the interface — the lobby, the HUD, the boards in the arena and the pause card. The steel, the rope and the room do not change. In the headset: pause, thumbstick right, and it is the last row.

Radio

Quest Browser has no speech engine, so the lines come as a voice-shaped buzz with the words on the radio's display and the boards. On a desktop with system voices, each speaker gets their own.

Feet

The headset only guesses where your feet are. Planted means a walking foot steps over the rope instead of catching it; the print turns gold when it counts.

Knots

Full turns around the rail that count as a clove hitch. Two is the knot.

Walking

Push the stick forward and you walk that way. Controller-relative lets you look around while you walk a straight line down the beam.

Hands

Audio

  1. Gear — pockets empty, then the harness: shoulders, chest clip, belt, leg clips. Helmet on. Everything else clipped into the harness. Radio check. Grab the rope and go.
  2. Rope — send it down whenever you like, even before you gear up; something has to hold the working end after that. A clove hitch and a half hitch on the catwalk rail frees your hands. Clip your 6' double lanyard to the lifeline or the top rail before you touch the beam.
  3. PCD — untie if you tied off, keep hold of the rope (or stand on it), walk out to the sling, hang the PCD, feed the rope the right way.
  4. Haul — on the ground rigger's call: leg to the apex, together to the hook eye, then the load, matching the other rigger's pace.
  5. Beam — burlap out, wrap the beam, mate it (Pin · Rope · Cable · Shackle), burlap under, steel down, square up, check for fouls.
  6. Point check — radio down.
Grip
Hold the grip button to hold things — gear, any part of the rope, a leg of steel. Let go over the void and it goes in
Harness
Bring it to your chest to shoulder it, then trigger at the chest, the belly, and each thigh to clip it. Seven rings: back, chest, belly, both hips, both rear. Gear clips to any ring; the lanyard hangs off the back ring and its hooks park on the shoulder straps
Hooks
Grip a hook and the trigger works its gate: near the rail or the lifeline it clips; already clipped, it comes off in your hand. Six feet of webbing is six feet — it tugs you back on the deck, and in a fall it is what the blowout pack pays out of: about six feet of free fall, two feet of pack, then you hang and swing in
Rope
It is real: 150 feet of it, every foot simulated. Hold it anywhere, let it swing, feel it go taut. Pull too hard against a taut line and it slips through your hand
Trigger
Act on what is under your hand: pick a program, dress a knot, hang the PCD, mate a shackle
Knots
Hold the rope end at the rail and circle your hand around the rail — the game counts the turns. Trigger to dress it
Haul
Grip the rope below the PCD and pull down, hand over hand. The heavier it gets, the harder you have to squeeze (trigger or grip) or it slips; hold weight too long and your hand shakes, then lets go — rest it on the cam
B / Y
Key the radio — with the radio in your hand. Listen first: everybody in the building is on this channel, and keying up over someone is stepping on their call
Thumbstick
Push it and you walk where that controller points (or where you look — Settings). Hold A / X 0.6 s to pause
Feet
Where the headset says they are. Stand on the rope to hold it; step off the beam and gravity has you
Desktop
Mouse look · WASD walk · left mouse = grip · right mouse / F = trigger · mouse wheel wraps a knot or hauls · E reach · R radio · P pause
